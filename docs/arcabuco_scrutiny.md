## The fork, restated with measurements

The old framing was "cool-but-steep vs warm-and-flat". Measured, that is only half right.

- **Terrain — nearly a tie.** In the elevation band where parcels actually trade, median slope is **10.2° in Moniquirá vs 10.5° in Arcabuco**. What differs is the tail: Arcabuco holds **2.6× more genuinely steep land** (12.5% vs 4.8% above 25°). So the highland is not steep *on average* — it just has more bad pockets, which means **parcel selection matters far more there**. The "Arcabuco is steep" impression comes from the whole-area figure (27.4% >25°), which includes the Iguaque massif where nothing is for sale.
- **Water — favours Arcabuco, and it is the one real gap.** Both flanks get essentially the same rain (1,826 vs 1,860 mm). Moniquirá is 4.8 °C warmer, so PET runs 1,421 vs 1,233 mm and an El Niño drought takes it to **10 deficit months against Arcabuco's 3**. In a normal year Moniquirá has *zero* deficit months and a 439 mm surplus — it is genuinely humid. The gap is drought resilience, not annual rainfall.
- **Services — favours Moniquirá, decisively.** A **Nivel I+II regional hospital in town** (118 servicios habilitados), which is also the nearest second-level care for the highland veredas (Arcabuco 19.8 km, Gachantivá 13.6 km — both closer than Tunja).
- **Cost — a tie.** G/Papayal at ~4,300 COP/m² and C/La Hoya at ~4,200 are the two cheapest parcels found, one on each flank.

Gating constraints, now differentiated: **Arcabuco** — SFF Iguaque + delimited páramo (no-build; the wettest parcels flirt with the edge) and UAF ≈9.9 ha. **Moniquirá** — no park, no páramo, no heritage overlay, but 13 municipal *Conservación Hídrica* reserves, the Serranía El Peligro edge, UAF 9.38 ha, and rural water that is **abundant but not potable** (all four monitored aqueducts IRCA "Riesgo Alto", none with treatment or disinfection).

## Cross-cutting

The decisive unknown has moved. It is no longer "is the highland too steep" — both flanks are mostly gentle where parcels trade. It is now **(a)** on the highland, *which* parcel, because the steep tail is real; and **(b)** on the warm flank, *water treatment and drought planning*, because abundance in a normal year is not resilience in a dry one. Every parcel still needs geotech, hydrogeology + a Corpoboyacá concession, protected-area overlay, title (SNR/ANT/UAF) and access checks — plus, in both municipalities, confirmation of the **municipality on the matrícula**: Moniquirá has its own veredas named "La Hoya" and "La Capilla", the labels under which C and F were filed.
