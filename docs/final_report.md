# Retirement Hacienda — Site Selection Analysis

### Colombian Central Highlands (Cundiboyacense) · 30-year horizon · Final report

**Date:** 19 July 2026 · **Prepared for:** Diego · **Scope:** El Colegio / Mesitas del Colegio (Cundinamarca) · Villa de Leyva (Boyacá) · Tunja (Boyacá), with candidate sectors surfaced within each.

> This is decision-support, not legal, engineering, or financial advice. Title work needs a Colombian abogado/notario; any parcel needs on-site geotechnical, hydrogeological and structural surveys before purchase or construction. Every quantitative claim traces to a cited source (Phase 0/1 reports + methods log); values that could not be reached from primary sources in this environment are flagged "needs field/primary pull," not invented.

## 1. The recommendation, in one page

**Base the hacienda on the wet north-east flank of the Ricaurte valley — the Arcabuco / Gachantivá / Santa Sofía sector — rather than in Villa de Leyva's town belt.** This sector wins the weighted scorecard under *every* priority weighting tested (default, climate-first, water-first, services-first, cost-first), because it resolves the single make-or-break constraint the whole brief kept circling back to — **water** — while also being the cheapest land, keeping a mild highland climate, sitting in a low-crime province, and carrying none of Villa de Leyva's heritage-construction limits. It is ~40–60 minutes from Tunja's Nivel III hospital and full city services, and ~3 hours from Bogotá / El Dorado.

**Keep two live alternatives, chosen by what you weight most:**

- **If flying in and out easily is paramount** (you're Europe-based, several trips a year), **El Colegio / Mesitas (Tequendama)** is the counter-pick: ~1 h 47 to El Dorado — decisively the best airport access — plus a genuinely pleasant warm ~23 °C templado climate and Bogotá's hospitals ~1.5 h away. The price is real and confirmed: **high landslide susceptibility and chronic rainy-season closures** on both Bogotá access corridors, plus peri-Bogotá property-crime spillover (finca theft). Viable *if* a parcel's own slope and access are geotechnically sound.
- **Villa de Leyva's town belt only if water is fully solved and budget is high.** It has the best climate and beauty of anything here, but it is water-constrained (a dry-subhumid valley that rationed in 2016 and is severely El Niño-exposed), the most expensive land, heritage-limited, and carries a 2026 construction-licensing-corruption flag. You get more of what makes Villa de Leyva attractive — the climate, the landscape, proximity to the town — by buying on the wetter flank a short drive away.

**Tunja is the services/hospital anchor, not the residence:** safest of the three, best everyday services, strongest road pipeline, but cold (~13 °C) and ~2.5–3 h from an international airport.

**Top parcels to pursue** (real current listings, all water/access claims still to be field-verified): Arcabuco / Peñas Blancas (COP 400 M, 3 ha, quebrada + potable tank, 1.3 km paved) as the best all-round wet-flank pick; Gachantivá / La Hoya (COP 230 M, 5.5 ha, forest + springs) as the cheapest larger parcel; and, if you prioritise airport access instead, Tena / Catalamonte (COP 1,750 M, 24 ha, two springs + two streams, ~25 min from El Dorado) — subject to a slope survey.

**Hold the group's land in a Colombian SAS** (not direct copropiedad): a partner exits by transferring *shares*, not by re-notarising the land, there's no partition-forced-sale risk, and shares sit outside the non-resident wealth-tax base. At the parcel sizes here (all < COP 2 bn) wealth-tax exposure is effectively nil.

## 3. What the evidence says, criterion by criterion

**Climate.** A real warm→mild→cold gradient, but corrected from the brief: Mesitas/El Colegio is a pleasant **~23 °C templado mean** (the "25–28 °C" was daytime highs), not a hot lowland; Villa de Leyva ~17–18 °C mild and sunny; the wet flank ~14–16 °C (cooler, cloudier); Tunja cold ~13 °C. Warming to mid-century is **+1.4 to +2.4 °C** (IDEAM ensemble) with an upper CMIP6/SSP5-8.5 bracket to ~+3.5 °C — enough to make Tunja marginally more comfortable and to nudge Mesitas toward the mid-20s, never into oppressive heat.

**Water — the decisive criterion.** A radiation-aware (Hargreaves) monthly water balance on IDEAM-anchored normals, with an El Niño drought stress test, quantifies what the brief flagged qualitatively:

| Sector | Annual P (mm) | Aridity index P/PET | Class | Deficit months | Under El Niño (P×0.6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arcabuco (wet flank)** | 1,827 | **1.48** | humid, +609 mm surplus | 1 | still only 3 deficit months |
| El Colegio / Mesitas | 1,168 | 0.73 | sub-humid (high PET from warmth) | 9 | 12 months |
| Villa de Leyva (town) | 1,006 | 0.63 | dry-subhumid | 10 | 12 months, ~990 mm deficit |
| Tunja | 653 | 0.50 | semi-arid | 12 | 12 months |
| Sáchica (dry SW) | 726 | 0.43 | semi-arid | 12 | 12 months |

The wet flank has a genuine surplus and barely flinches under drought; every valley-floor option runs a chronic seasonal deficit and goes fully dry in an El Niño year — precisely the mechanism behind Villa de Leyva's 2016 rationing.

![Monthly water balance — precipitation vs radiation-aware PET; red fill = deficit](WBFIG) Governance: any use of water needs a **Corpoboyacá/CAR concession**; on the over-subscribed Villa de Leyva side a groundwater concession + storage is the realistic path, and any parcel must sit outside protected **rondas hídricas** and the **SFF Iguaque** buffer.

**Seismic.** All three municipalities are **NSR-10 intermediate** (not high) and volcano-free — confirmed. Computed distance to the nearest cataloged active fault (GEM database) is **≥26 km everywhere** (Tequendama nearer the Usme/Honda faults at 26–35 km; Boyacá sites 52–64 km from Alto del Trigo), so no site sits on an active trace. Tunja is at the top of the intermediate band (Aa 0.20). Build to NSR-10 with intermediate (DMO) detailing — a moderate (~+5–15 %) cost premium — plus a mandatory site soil study. Exact Aa/Av for El Colegio and Villa de Leyva still need a direct read of NSR-10 Apéndice A-4.

**Landslide & access.** This is Mesitas' signature risk and it is **confirmed, not overstated**: both Bogotá access corridors (Mosquera–La Mesa and the Tequendama descent) suffer recurrent rainy-season closures — Km 74 "Pantanos", peaje El Charquito, Mondoñedo — with a fault-controlled 2008 disaster in-municipality (vereda Antioqueñita, 47 houses). SGC susceptibility is high; extreme-rainfall is projected to intensify (~2× the 100-year event by mid-century), which raises triggering frequency. The two corridors are each other's detour, so total isolation is unlikely, but rainy-season delay is a near-certainty. The Boyacá sites (valley floor and flank) are far less exposed.

**Natural resources.** All have high solar potential. The wet flank and Tequendama have the best soils/water for productive land; the semi-arid valley floors (Villa de Leyva, Sáchica, Tunja) are scenic but drier.

**Security & governance.** All three sit in the safer half of Colombia with no armed-group presence, against a *nationally* worsening backdrop (Paz Total collapsed; a hardline government takes office 7 Aug 2026). Concrete buyer-relevant risks differ: **Tunja** safest (4 homicides in 2025, lowest of any capital); **Villa de Leyva** decade-low department homicides but persistent tourist-town property crime, a **Feb-2025 targeted robbery-murder of a US expat**, and a **May-2026 mayor jailing over construction-licensing bribery** (a permitting-integrity risk); **El Colegio/Mesitas** in Colombia's safest department but with peri-Bogotá organized-crime spillover and documented **finca theft** (an Aug-2025 massacre at a recreational finca). The wet-flank rural sectors largely avoid the tourist-town crime.

**Economy & cost.** Land prices (real listings, COP/m²): wet flank **cheapest at ~4,000–18,000**; Tequendama's Tena/San Antonio **7,000–38,000**; El Colegio/Mesitas **~49,000–130,000**; Villa de Leyva town belt **most expensive, median ~110,000, to 390,000** near town. Carrying costs: predial is a municipally-set 5–16‰ of (below-market) cadastral value — typically ~0.1–0.4 %/yr of market; a Villa de Leyva cadastral update will push its base up. **Wealth tax is effectively nil** for any parcel here (all below even the lower ~COP 2.1 bn threshold; non-residents are taxed only on Colombian-situated net assets).

**Vital services & airport.** Hospitals by level (REPS): **Tunja** Nivel III in-city (only 3rd-level in Boyacá); **La Mesa** Nivel II ~15 min from Mesitas; **Villa de Leyva** Nivel I local, Nivel III at Tunja ~50 min. Airport is where the geography splits: **Mesitas ~1 h 47 to El Dorado** vs **~3 h** from the Boyacá sites (Paipa offers only intermittent domestic flights). For Europe-based owners this is the strongest single argument for the Tequendama side.

**Development trajectory.** Strongest in **Tunja/BTS** (mature Bogotá–Tunja dual carriageway + new Tunja–Duitama opening 2026–27 + Paipa air service). **Villa de Leyva/Ricaurte** improving (Villa de Leyva–Arcabuco paving completed Sep 2025 — a direct plus for the wet flank — plus tourism-driven value growth, though water caps buildable growth). **Tequendama's** flagship road program is defensive stabilisation of the landslide corridor, not a transformational upgrade.

**Legal, tenure & co-ownership.** Foreigners/non-residents may own rural land freely. The due-diligence spine: title chain via SNR/VUR (watch for *falsa tradición*), an ANT baldío/UAF check (a group buy can trip the one-UAF accumulation cap if the parcel traces to a baldío), POT zoning (buildable vs forest reserve / páramo / ronda hídrica / Iguaque buffer), Villa de Leyva heritage reach on rural parcels, and the water concession. **Recommended vehicle: a Colombian SAS** holding the land, with a shareholders' agreement — clean exits, no partition risk, shares outside the wealth-tax base; a shareholder's death still triggers a Colombian succession of the *shares* (soften with lifetime nuda-propiedad transfers or a foreign holdco). Avoid plain copropiedad as the long-term structure.

## 4. Per-site fact sheets

### ★ Ricaurte wet NE flank — Arcabuco / Gachantivá / Santa Sofía (RECOMMENDED)
*Elevation ~2,400–2,600 m · ~14–16 °C · Boyacá, Ricaurte province*
- **Why it wins:** best water security by a wide margin (AI 1.48, surplus, drought-resilient); cheapest buildable land (~4–18k COP/m²); low-crime rural province; no heritage overlay; paved Villa de Leyva–Arcabuco link completed 2025; ~30–45 min to Villa de Leyva's amenities, ~40–60 min to Tunja's Nivel III hospital.
- **Trade-offs:** cooler and cloudier than the valley floor; thinner local services; ~3 h to El Dorado; hillier terrain (verify parcel slope); confirm UAF/baldío status and Iguaque-buffer/ronda-hídrica clearance.
- **Verdict:** the best balance of the ten criteria for a 30-year, water-secure, affordable hacienda with a mild climate.

### El Colegio / Mesitas del Colegio — Tequendama (BEST FOR AIRPORT ACCESS)
*Elevation ~990–1,300 m · ~23 °C templado · Cundinamarca*
- **Strengths:** decisively closest to El Dorado (~1 h 47) and Bogotá services (~1.5 h to Nivel III/IV); pleasant warm climate; deep, liquid land market with condominio-friendly zoning; mid prices (Tena/San Antonio cheaper).
- **Risks (confirmed):** high landslide susceptibility + chronic rainy-season access closures; peri-Bogotá organized-crime/finca-theft; high evaporative demand gives a real dry-season deficit despite decent rainfall.
- **Verdict:** the pick if airport access and warmth outrank everything — *conditional on* a parcel with sound slope, secure access and its own water.

### Villa de Leyva town belt (BEAUTY, IF WATER SOLVED & BUDGET HIGH)
*Elevation ~2,149 m · ~17–18 °C · Boyacá*
- **Strengths:** best climate and landscape here; strong tourism amenity and appreciation; flat, low-landslide valley floor.
- **Risks:** water-constrained (dry-subhumid; 2016 rationing; severe El Niño exposure); most expensive land + highest cost of living; heritage/PEMP construction limits near the core; a 2026 licensing-corruption flag; over-subscribed water makes a concession harder.
- **Verdict:** better satisfied by buying on the wetter flank nearby; only pursue the town belt itself with a fully secured water source and a high budget.

### Tunja (SERVICES/HOSPITAL ANCHOR, NOT RESIDENCE)
*Elevation ~2,780 m · ~13 °C · Boyacá capital*
- **Strengths:** safest option; Nivel III hospital + universities + full capital services; strongest infrastructure pipeline; low landslide exposure.
- **Risks:** cold for a warm-climate retirement; driest of the three (semi-arid; a rural parcel needs its own source); ~2.5–3 h to El Dorado, no commercial airport.
- **Verdict:** the everyday-services and healthcare anchor for a hacienda placed nearby (esp. the wet flank), not the build site itself.

## 6. Next steps (before any offer)

1. **Shortlist 2–3 parcels** on the wet flank (start with Arcabuco/Peñas Blancas and Gachantivá/La Hoya) and, if airport access is weighted heavily, one Tequendama option (Tena/Catalamonte).
2. **Commission local field checks:** a topographic + geotechnical survey (slope/stability), a hydrogeological assessment of the claimed water source, and confirmation the parcel sits outside protected buffers.
3. **Legal due diligence** via a Colombian abogado/notario: Certificado de Tradición y Libertad, ANT baldío/UAF check, POT land-use certificate, water-concession feasibility with Corpoboyacá/CAR, and (for Villa de Leyva parcels) heritage reach.
4. **Stand up the SAS + shareholders' agreement** with a tributarista before signing; register the foreign investment with Banco de la República to protect repatriation.
5. **Close the open data items** the sandbox couldn't reach: exact NSR-10 Aa/Av (Apéndice A-4) and precise SGC fault/susceptibility geometry for the chosen parcel.

## 7. Caveats, open items & reproducibility
