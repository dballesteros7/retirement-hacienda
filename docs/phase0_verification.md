# Phase 0 — Verification of Anchor Findings

### Retirement Hacienda Site Selection · Colombian Central Highlands

**Date:** 19 July 2026 · **Phase:** 0 of 3 (fact-check before deep analysis) · **Sites:** El Colegio / Mesitas del Colegio (Cundinamarca) · Villa de Leyva (Boyacá) · Tunja (Boyacá)

---

## Methodology & scope

Five parallel research streams cross-checked the brief's §3 "established findings" against primary/official Colombian sources (SGC, NSR-10/AIS, Corpoboyacá, ESVILLA, MinSalud REPS, ANI/INVÍAS, Mincultura) plus reputable secondary sources and dated news. Every datum is cited with source + URL + access date (all **accessed 19 Jul 2026**). Where a primary value could not be reached — several SGC/IGAC portals block automated access or sit behind broken TLS — the item is marked **UNVERIFIED** with the exact source needed, never guessed. Precise geospatial re-derivation (site PGA, buildable-slope polygons, water balance, drive-time isochrones) is Phase 1 work; Phase 0 only establishes whether the brief's premises hold.

**Bottom line:** The brief's core structure — three volcano-free, *intermediate*-seismic highland sites forming a warm → mild → cold climate gradient, each with one signature risk — **holds up well.** Four claims need correction (most importantly, Mesitas is *templado ~23 °C*, not "warm 25–28 °C"); the Villa de Leyva **water constraint is real and, if anything, under-stated**; and the Mesitas **landslide/access risk is strongly confirmed**. A short list of exact values (per-site Aa/Av, fault distances, precipitation totals) still needs a primary-source pull and is logged for Phase 1.

## The four corrections that matter

**1. Mesitas is *pleasantly temperate*, not hot.** The brief's "25–28 °C" is the daytime-high band; the annual **mean is ~19–23 °C** (23 °C at the 990 m seat, cooler on the higher slopes). This is *tierra templada* — arguably a nicer year-round climate than the brief implied, but it changes the climate scoring: Mesitas is the warm end of the gradient, not a hot lowland. It also matters for the warming analysis in Phase 1 (a templado site at ~1,000 m warms toward the low-to-mid 20s, not into oppressive heat).

**2. Villa de Leyva's water story is the real make-or-break — and it's tighter than stated.** The town runs on roughly **54 L/s of concessioned supply** (Río Cane 34 L/s + La Colorada 5 L/s + a 15 L/s well opened in 2020) for ~30,000 residents plus a heavy weekend/holiday tourist load, on a **semi-arid valley floor (~997 mm/yr)**. Formal rationing (3 h/day cuts) was imposed in the 2016 drought, and the 2020–2023 municipal plan states supply quality drops "principalmente en temporada alta." The thesis isn't just confirmed — it's the single most important constraint on any Villa-de-Leyva parcel. **A parcel here is only viable with its own secured, Corpoboyacá-concessioned source** (groundwater + storage is likely more reliable than surface capture), ideally on the **wetter NE flank** (Arcabuco / Santa Sofía / Gachantivá / Iguaque foothills) and **outside protected rondas hídricas and the SFF Iguaque buffer**.

**3. Boyacá is farther from Bogotá than the brief's drive-times suggest.** Tunja→Bogotá on the BTS is **~2 h 20 min free-flow and 2.5–3 h in typical traffic**, not 1.5 h; Villa de Leyva→Bogotá is **~3 h typical**. The *internal* logic still holds (Villa de Leyva → Tunja services anchor is a genuine ~50 min), but the "Boyacá is close to Bogotá/El Dorado" premise is weaker than framed. This **strengthens the airport-access case for Mesitas** (~1 h 47 min to BOG) for Europe-based owners flying in and out.

**4. The Villa de Leyva water-supply numbers were slightly conflated.** Río Cane's *river concession* is **34 L/s**; the **40 L/s** figure is the "La Diana" treatment-plant capacity. The 5 L/s (La Colorada) and 15 L/s (well) figures are correct; the well's "San Roque" name could not be confirmed in official sources (it's referred to only as the *pozo profundo*, inaugurated Nov 2020). Directionally the brief was right; the exact ledger is above.

## Domain findings

### Seismic & geology — ✅ premises confirmed

- **Seismic zone.** NSR-10 zone thresholds (from max of Aa, Av): Baja ≤ 0.10, **Intermedia 0.10–0.20**, Alta > 0.20. **Tunja = Aa 0.20 / Av 0.20** (confirmed from NSR-10 Título A, Tabla A.2.3-2) — the top of the intermediate band and the highest demand of the three. **El Colegio and Villa de Leyva** are both intermediate by regional evidence (Cundinamarca's centre-west and central Boyacá are intermediate; the *high* municipalities sit on the department flanks — Middle-Magdalena and Llanos piedmont). Their **exact Aa/Av are UNVERIFIED** — they live only in NSR-10 Apéndice A-4 and the SGC portal (`amenazasismica.sgc.gov.co`), both egress-blocked this pass. → *Phase-1 action.*
- **Volcano-free.** Confirmed. Nevado del Ruiz (4.89 °N, 75.32 °W) and Cerro Machín are ~100–106 km west of El Colegio and ~215–230 km SW of the Boyacá sites, across the Magdalena valley; their lahars channel west/southwest, away from all three. Only conceivable effect is nuisance ashfall over Bogotá (possible airport disruption), not structural hazard.
- **Active faults.** Boyacá & Soapaga faults (transpressive, "potencialmente activas") pass ~30–50 km and ~40–70 km E/NE of Villa de Leyva/Tunja, with Boyacá-fault splays near Tunja (Chivatá); the 1646 Firavitoba ~M6–6.5 event is associated with this system. For El Colegio, the Bituima–Cambao–Vianí western thrust front lies ~15–55 km W/NW with low slip (0.1–1 mm/yr). A once-claimed near-Bogotá active fault ("La Cajita") was refuted in the literature as a landslide. Exact distances/slip-rates need the **SGC 2020 active-faults database** → *Phase-1 action.*
- **Deep source.** The Bucaramanga/Los Santos seismic nest (~150 km depth, ~130–150 km NNE of the Boyacá sites) is a real, recognised intermediate-depth source in the national model — felt widely but attenuated by depth.
- **Implication.** Intermediate zone → moderate design accelerations (Aa ≈ 0.15–0.20 g) with intermediate ductility detailing (DMO), not the special detailing (DES) and higher forces of Alta zones — a roughly **+5–15 % sismo-resistant premium** over a non-seismic baseline, provided a Título H soil study is done (soft/alluvial soils amplify shaking regardless of zone).

### Climate & elevation — ✅ confirmed (one correction)

| Site | Elevation (seat) | Mean annual T | Annual precip | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| El Colegio / Mesitas | **990 m** (municipality ~600–1,800 m+) | **~19–23 °C** (not 25–28) | *Unverified* (regionally ~1,200–2,000 mm, bimodal) | Tierra templada |
| Villa de Leyva | **2,149 m** | ~17–18 °C (18.1 municipal) | **~997 mm** | Mild, **semi-arid** valley floor |
| Tunja | ~2,720–2,820 m | ~13 °C | ~700–1,360 mm (*uncertain*) | Tierra fría |

Rainfall is bimodal at all three (Mar–May and Oct–Nov peaks). Villa de Leyva's valley floor is a documented dry enclave (a desert sector sits ~1 km from town; the Desierto de la Candelaria ~32 km SW), with markedly wetter flanks rising NE into the Iguaque páramo (Laguna de Iguaque, 3,800 m). **Data flags for Phase 1:** climate-data.org's El Colegio precipitation (9,311 mm) is corrupt — reject; its Villa de Leyva temperature (13.4 °C) is cold-biased — use the municipal ~17–18 °C. El Colegio and Tunja precipitation totals need a clean IDEAM/CAR station.

### Water security, Villa de Leyva — ✅ confirmed (central constraint)

Covered in "corrections" above. Governance: **Corpoboyacá** is the environmental authority (CAR) granting **concesiones de aguas** (Decreto 1076/2015; holder pays the *tasa por utilización*) and defining **rondas hídricas** (protected buffer up to 30 m plus a conservation strip; Ley 1450/2011, Decreto 2245/2017). SFF Iguaque already carries ~19 water concessions serving ~50,000 people — the resource is spoken for, which makes an *own, legally-secured* source non-negotiable for a parcel here.

### Landslide & access, Mesitas / Tequendama — ✅ confirmed

Two paved corridors from Bogotá both suffer rainy-season closures: **Route A** (Bogotá–Mosquera–Mondoñedo–La Mesa) and **Route B** (the Tequendama descent via peaje El Charquito). Documented, dated closures include Km 74 "Pantanos"/La Gran Vía (Dec 2017 & Jan 2018 — a *recurring* point), El Charquito derrumbe (Mar 2025), and ~10 slides on Mosquera–La Mesa with multi-day closures (Apr 2026). In-municipality, a **fault-controlled mass movement destroyed 47 houses in vereda Antioqueñita (Nov 2008)**. El Colegio's own EOT flags widespread *geoinestabilidad*; SGC susceptibility is qualitatively **high**. Redeeming nuance: the two routes are each other's official detour, so total isolation is unlikely — but same-storm double closures have happened. **No official annual closure statistic exists** (would need an INVÍAS/UNGRD records survey). Exact susceptibility % and a candidate-parcel slope read are *Phase-1 geospatial work.*

Hospitals (MinSalud REPS): **La Mesa** — ESE Pedro León Álvarez Díaz, **Nivel II** (Tequendama referral); **Tunja** — Hospital Universitario San Rafael, **Nivel III** (the only third-level in Boyacá, teaching hospital, new high-complexity tower under construction); **Villa de Leyva** — ESE San Francisco, **Nivel I** (modern but basic). No commercial airport at Villa de Leyva or Tunja; El Dorado is the international gateway (Paipa offers intermittent *domestic* regional flights ~1 h from Villa de Leyva). **BTS** = Briceño–Tunja–Sogamoso doble calzada (~206 km, ANI concession 377/2002, now in O&M). Villa de Leyva's heritage status imposes real build constraints (colonial materials/colours/heights, heritage permits) in the historic centre + influence zone via a PEMP; whether rural surroundings are similarly constrained runs through the municipal POT and is **UNVERIFIED** → *Phase-1/legal action.*

## Recalibrated working hypothesis

The brief's hypothesis — **base near Villa de Leyva for climate/beauty, with Tunja (~50 min) as the services/hospital anchor; counter-case is airport access favouring Mesitas** — survives Phase 0, with three refinements:

1. **Villa de Leyva is viable only with a secured own-water source** on the wetter NE flank or proven groundwater — this becomes the first filter on any parcel, ahead of price or view.
2. **The airport counter-case for Mesitas is stronger than the brief implied**, because Boyacá↔Bogotá is a ~3 h drive, not ~1.5–2 h. For Europe-based owners flying in/out several times a year, that gap compounds.
3. **Mesitas' landslide/access risk is confirmed and real**, but its climate is a pleasant templado and its two access routes give partial redundancy — so it stays a live contender, not a scratch, pending a parcel-level slope/geotech read.

Tunja itself remains the "services, not residence" option — best hospital and everyday services, but cold (~13 °C) and the farthest everyday drive to an international airport.
