## Moniquirá — the warm flank

### H · lote with geotech done — **the best building land found**
*8.0 ha · COP 720 M (9,000/m²) · Casa 360 · 15 photos*

The photographs are unambiguous: **open, gently-sloping ridgetop pasture**, closely grazed, fenced with post-and-wire, scattered mature spreading trees hung with Spanish moss and bromeliads, and a genuine panorama over a wide valley to distant blue ranges. No rock outcrops, no ravines, no scrub. Several frames show parkland-like ground where you could site a house without earthworks.

Listing: 15 potreros, **aljibe + pozo**, easy connection to power and gas, fully fenced, 5 km access of which 3 km paved, 15 min to town, and it is **handed over with a geotechnical study of the soil and of the water**. That study is the single most valuable document in this entire set — it is the thing every other parcel would have to pay for.

**Read:** if the group wants to build four houses with the least uncertainty, this is the parcel. The premium over G (1.85×) buys away most of the diligence risk.

### G · Papayal — **cheapest usable land, but the water claim is the weak point**
*9.11 ha · COP 390 M (4,279/m²) · ATOS · 10 photos*

The photos support part of the story and quietly undercut the rest. Genuinely good: a **broad flat valley-bottom pasture** (G01) and gentle silvopasture grazing perhaps ten head of cattle under mature shade trees (G03). And the "quebrada natural" is real — G07 shows a clear stream running over cobbles in deep shade, the kind that looks perennial.

But two frames change the picture. **G05 is a deep ravine with near-vertical rock walls**, and **G09 an exposed limestone outcrop with a cave-like recess** — this is **karst**. G10 shows a steep forested cone rising directly behind the pasture. Against that, "el 90% del terreno es aprovechable" is a seller's number, not an observation: a meaningful share is gorge and steep forest.

Karst is not a deal-breaker but it changes the engineering. Limestone country means possible voids and sinkholes under foundations, groundwater that moves fast and unpredictably through fissures, and **septic fields that can reach the aquifer far quicker than in clay**. Combined with the Papayal vereda aqueduct being IRCA "Riesgo Alto" with no treatment, water on G needs designing, not assuming.

**Read:** the best price per usable hectare, with the largest engineering unknown. Worth a geotechnical and hydrogeological survey *before* an offer, not after.

### N · finca panelera — **41 ha, working infrastructure, above the UAF**
*40.96 ha · COP 1,700 M (4,150/m²) · Inmobiliaria Fénix · 9 photos*

The description is much richer than the summary line suggested: *"HERMOSA FINCA PANELERA"*, 64 fanegas of **semi-flat terrain**, acueducto veredal plus **its own aljibes**, renewed cane plantings, *"suelos muy buenos"*, and a complete working **trapiche**: mill structure, fondos, moulding room, hornilla, bagacera. Plus a module with bedroom, dining room, kitchen and bathroom, and **two simple houses**. 9 km from town on a vereda road *"en buen estado"*.

The photos bear this out — N08 shows a well-made gravel road running beside fenced sugarcane on flat ground; N05 is the interior of the moulding room, tiled and plumbed; N02 a maintained access road through rolling green country.

**Read:** at the same COP/m² as G but 4.5× the land, this is the only candidate **above the 9.38 ha UAF**, so the only one that could be lawfully subdivided into individually-titled family plots instead of held whole in an SAS. It also arrives with buildings, water and an income-producing crop. Fourteen months unsold at 1,700 M invites an offer.

### K · Neval y Cruces — **individually titled lots, services already in**
*5,000–8,821 m² each · COP 170 M for 5,000 m² (~34,000/m²) · Iván F. Botía · 19 photos*

K01 is a **drone shot** and the most informative single image in the set: gently rolling green smallholder country, a red-roofed farmhouse, banana groves, small fields, and the 7,400 m² lot outlined. K05 shows flat pasture with a **pond, a building, and power lines overhead** — the grid is already there.

Five lots: 5,000 · 5,357 · 6,000 · 7,400 (with a 100 m² house and huerta) · 8,821 m² (with **its own nacedero**). Services listed as electricity, acueducto, gas, water and quebrada. **Each lot has its own matrícula inmobiliaria.** 4 km from town, 2.8 km paved.

**Read:** the cleanest answer to the group's ownership problem — each family could own its own titled plot outright, no SAS required, with the 8,821 m² spring lot as the shared water asset. The costs are density (this is a populated landscape with close neighbours, not seclusion) and price per m², ~8× G's. Verify the subdivision was lawfully constituted before anything else.

### M · near vía Togüi — **the most drought-proof water of any candidate**
*18 ha · COP 1,860 M (10,333/m²) · URBI Soluciones · 15 photos*

Photographs show an established, well-kept property: mown lawns, mature ornamental planting, a tile-roofed house with a covered terrace, grain drying on tarps. Infrastructure is extensive — 350 m² cabaña, 10 parking spaces, staff house, a shed for 600 hens, pig facility, fodder-chopping room, half a fanegada of citrus and plantain, 15 potreros, 4 tilapia ponds and **its own transformer**.

The detail that matters most: **"3 nacimientos de agua propio (veranera)"**. *Veranera* means they run through the dry season. Given that Moniquirá's single measured weakness is drought resilience — 10 deficit months under El Niño against Arcabuco's 3 — a spring that does not fail in summer is worth more here than anywhere else in the search.

**Read:** expensive, but it is the only candidate that directly answers the one thing the water balance says is wrong with the warm flank.

### L · Alto Cantando — **a granted water concession and a real income crop**
*7.76 ha · COP 1,070 M (13,798/m²) · Nelson Mestizo · 14 photos*

L03 shows the business: **dozens of crates of harvested guava** in an orchard, feeding Moniquirá's bocadillo industry. The listing's 2,500 guava trees are evidently real and productive. House of 3 bedrooms and 2 bathrooms, acueducto veredal, and — the reason to care — **an existing concesión de agua on the quebrada**. That is the permission Corpoboyacá is on record as being cautious about granting anew in this drainage.

**Read:** buying an existing concession is materially easier than applying for one. Pricey per m² and only 7.76 ha, but it removes the hardest permission from the critical path.

### E · San Cristóbal — **and the listing is mis-tagged**
*8.5 ha · COP 750 M (8,824/m²) · ATOS · 8 photos*

⚠ **The URL and every prior version of this dossier place E in vereda Neval y Cruces. The listing text says "vereda San Cristóbal".** Those are different veredas. This is precisely the portal mis-tagging this project has caught repeatedly — verify on the matrícula, and treat any Neval y Cruces attribution for E as unconfirmed.

E03 is a genuinely spectacular 20–30 m waterfall dropping through mossy cloud forest. But note the text describes only *"quebrada lindante"*, pozos, nacederos and an acueducto point — **no waterfall is claimed in words**, so whether that fall is on-title or a neighbouring landmark is an open question. The terrain around it is steep and forested.

**Read:** demoted. Wrong vereda on record, a headline photo that the text does not substantiate, and steep ground. Ask ATOS for the matrícula before spending more time.

### O · Colorado — **small, enclosed, and in a wildfire-recurrent vereda**
*1.63 ha · COP 320 M (19,671/m²) · ATOS · 19 photos*

O04 shows a narrow grassy strip hemmed by dense secondary forest on both sides — little open ground. The listing offers an own nacimiento and an abundant quebrada, 400 m from the national road. But 1.63 ha cannot hold four houses with any privacy, and **Colorado is named in the municipal PDM as a wildfire-recurrent vereda**.

**Read:** out for the group use-case.

## Arcabuco & Gachantivá — the cool highland

### A · Peñas Blancas with house — **the only turnkey option on this flank**
*2.97 ha · COP 400 M · RGD / Rogelio Gómez · 30 photos*

Richer than previously recorded: **1.3 km from the paved Arcabuco–Moniquirá road (3 minutes by car), 5 km from the main plaza.** Water by quebrada plus a **potable reserve tank**; mains electricity; septic well; bottled gas. The house is a 4-bedroom, 2-bathroom casa campestre with a fully-fitted kitchen and a living-dining room **with a fireplace**, plus a storage room and wraparound eaves. The agent pitches cabaña or glamping income via Airbnb.

Caveat on the gallery: a good number of the 30 photos are of **Arcabuco's town plaza and church**, not the parcel — attractive context (the town is genuinely handsome) but it pads the count.

**Read:** at 2.97 ha it is small for four houses, but it is the only candidate where the group could sleep on site from week one while building. Best drought resilience of any option.

### C · La Hoya — **beautiful, watered, and harder to clear than it looks**
*5.45 ha · COP 230 M (4,216/m²) · ATOS · 21 photos*

C08 confirms the water: a spring emerging from an eroded bank into a green pasture bowl ringed by dense forest. But **C03 is the decisive frame** — a wide expanse of **dense bracken fern regrowth** with scattered shrubs, backed by cloud forest. Bracken indicates acidic, leached, low-fertility ground and it is genuinely difficult to clear, because the rhizomes run deep and it returns.

Combined with the DEM finding that Arcabuco's band holds 2.6× more land above 25° than Moniquirá's, and that a *hoya* (basin) is exactly where steep ground concentrates, the "how much is actually buildable" question stands unresolved and is now the more worrying for being visible in the photos.

**Read:** the cheapest land per m² in the whole search and the most beautiful, but the clearing and soil-improvement bill is real and unquantified.

### B · Peñas Blancas blank — **dry-season browning, visible rock**
*2.43 ha · COP 270 M (11,102/m²) · ATOS · 15 photos*

B03 is instructive precisely because it is unglamorous: **tan, dry-season grass** on a gentle-to-moderate slope with **rock breaking the surface** and a forest edge behind. Set beside Moniquirá's uniformly green galleries, it is a visual confirmation of the water balance — the highland does brown off in its deficit month — and of the flagged clay/poor Peñas Blancas soils. Listing claims nacimiento, a bordering quebrada and reservorios.

**Read:** honest, cheap highland land next door to A. Small, and you are buying slope plus a farm track.

### D · Monte Suárez — **the finished house, and the boundary question**
*8.93 ha · COP 1,600 M · ATOS · 30 photos*

D04 shows a properly finished kitchen: varnished timber beams, gas hob, modern cabinetry, terracotta floor. The build is new — 240 m², 3 bedrooms, 3 bathrooms, living-dining, office, integrated kitchen, laundry — plus an older house for caretakers, a waterfall and a spring. ATOS explicitly offers **permuta (swap) against property in Bogotá or Tunja**.

*Small observation from the photograph:* stacked bottled-water jugs on the kitchen floor — consistent with the finding that rural supply here is not drunk untreated.

**Read:** unchanged. The most comfortable house in the set, at the highest price, with its head against SFF Iguaque. The protected-boundary answer decides whether this is a purchase or a non-starter, and it must come first.

## What changed versus the July pass

1. **E is in the wrong vereda** on the portal — San Cristóbal, not Neval y Cruces.
2. **G sits on karst.** Visible limestone outcrops, a cave recess and a deep rock ravine. Foundations, septic siting and groundwater all need specialist input, and "90% aprovechable" is not supported by the images.
3. **H is clearly the best building land**, not merely the lowest-risk one — the photographs show clean, open, gently-sloping ground with no outcrop or scrub.
4. **N is far more than a big cheap parcel** — it is a going panela concern with a trapiche, two houses, own aljibes and good soils, and it is the only candidate above the UAF.
5. **M has dry-season-reliable springs**, the direct antidote to the flank's one measured weakness.
6. **L comes with a water concession already granted.**
7. **C's clearing problem is now visible** — bracken fern over a large part of the parcel.
8. **Six candidates have sat unsold for a year with one agency**, and two listings volunteer that they are negotiable or open to a swap.
